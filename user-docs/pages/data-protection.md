# Data Protection & Authorisation

::: tip Face data is temporary. It lasts one service and is then deleted.
Kyro keeps **no photographs of anybody**. Each face becomes a list of numbers, and Kyro has no idea whose it is — no names, no record of who attended, and nothing is ever compared against a list of people.

Those numbers exist for one reason: so that somebody who steps out and comes back is not counted twice. They are deleted **when the service ends** — and also when a different service starts, the moment face matching is switched off, if the authorisation is withdrawn or passes its review date, and in any case **within twelve hours**, whether or not a service ever ended properly.

Nobody has to remember to delete anything. Kyro does it.
:::

This page is where your church writes down its permission to use face matching, and who decided it. You answer the questions once, and review them about once a year.

Kyro will not let face matching run until it is answered and approved. That is not a formality: a list of numbers that identifies a person is biometric data, and UK law treats it differently from counting heads.

::: danger Answering these is not the same as being compliant
Kyro will never tell you that your church is compliant, because it cannot know. That is a judgement about your church, your building and your congregation, made by people who are accountable for it.

What you write here is a record of your decisions, not a legal opinion — and ticking a box saying you have a DPIA is not the same as having done one. If you are unsure, take advice; the Information Commissioner's Office publishes free guidance for small organisations.
:::

## Where to find it

In Kyro, open **Data Protection** in the left-hand menu. It is there for administrators only, and it does not have to be switched on for anybody — an administrator always has it.

If you cannot see it, sign out and back in: the menu is built from your account's pages, and an account created before this page existed needs a fresh sign-in to pick it up.

## Why this exists

Kyro can count people without reading a single face, and it does that by default.

Face matching is an extra that solves one specific problem: telling whether the person who just walked back in is somebody already counted. Without it, an usher who holds the door all morning is counted every time they step through, and a choir in matching robes cannot be told apart at all.

To do that, Kyro turns each face into a list of 128 numbers. It keeps **no photograph**, and it has **no idea who anybody is** — no names, no records of who attended. But a list of numbers that identifies a person *is* biometric data, and under UK GDPR that needs:

- a **lawful basis** under Article 6 — the same as any other use of personal data; **and**
- a **separate condition** under Article 9, because biometric data is special category data.

The second one is the step most often missed. Article 6 on its own is not enough.

## What you have to answer

Eleven things. Most are a sentence or two. Each asks you to **write something**, not tick something — a box that only needs ticking records that somebody clicked, while a written answer records what you decided and can be read back by whoever has to defend it.

| | What it asks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The church, and who is responsible for the data | Someone accountable **by name**, not "the church" |
| 2 | Who is approving this, and the role that lets them | |
| 3 | **Lawful basis (Article 6)** — which, and why | In your own words |
| 4 | **Condition for biometric data (Article 9)** — which, and why | A second condition, *as well as* Article 6 |
| 5 | Consent arrangements, or why consent is not what you rely on | If it is consent, how it is asked for and how it is taken back |
| 6 | Data protection impact assessment | Required for biometric monitoring in a public space |
| 7 | What the notices say, and where they are | People must be told **before** they walk past the camera |
| 8 | What somebody who does not want to take part is offered | There must be a way to attend without being measured |
| 9 | How long face data is kept | You confirm it matches your privacy notice |
| 10 | Anyone else handling the data | And the agreement with them |
| 11 | When this will be looked at again | Within twelve months |

Anything missing is listed at the top of the page, with the reason it is needed.

## How long face data lives

Kyro keeps the numbers for **the length of one service**, so that people are still recognised if the system restarts mid-service. They are deleted:

- when the service ends;
- when a different service starts;
- the moment face matching is switched off;
- when this authorisation is withdrawn or passes its review date;
- and **within twelve hours whatever else happens** — even if a service never ended properly.

That last one matters. A service *can* fail to end, if a camera computer is switched off mid-morning. A promise about biometric data must not depend on everything else working.

## Approving it

When all eleven are answered, the responsible person approves it — **by name and role**, confirming each statement separately:

- I am authorised by the church to take this decision, and the church is accountable for it.
- I have read the DPIA and it covers face matching as Kyro uses it.
- The lawful basis and Article 9 condition above are the church's own, not an example.
- The notices described above are actually up at the entrances.
- Somebody who does not want to take part can attend, and the people on the door know how.

Only then can face matching be switched on.

::: warning Changing an approved answer removes the approval
An approval covers particular words. If those words change, it has to be approved again — by whoever is accountable, not by whoever happened to edit the page.
:::

## Review dates, and what happens when one passes

Every authorisation has a review date, and it cannot be more than twelve months away. An authorisation with no end is one nobody ever revisits.

When the date passes, **Kyro stops face matching by itself**, deletes the face codes, and says so on *Is Kyro working?* on the AI Count page. Nobody has to remember.

## Withdrawing it

**Withdraw authorisation** on that page stops face matching immediately — not at the end of the service — and deletes the face codes there and then. Use it if somebody objects, if something changes, or if you simply want it off while you think.

It can be lifted again afterwards without filling everything in a second time.

## The history

Every change is recorded — what changed, who changed it, and when — including somebody asking not to be included.

**No face data of any kind is in that history.** Keeping a face code to prove that somebody asked not to be measured would be keeping the very thing they asked Kyro not to hold.

## If you would rather not use face matching at all

That is a perfectly reasonable decision, and Kyro works without it.

You lose the ability to tell a returning person from a new one when no camera can see their face, so expect ushers and anybody stepping out and back to be counted more than once. Everything else — the headcount, arrivals and departures, the seat map, the rota — works exactly the same.

## Documents

Kyro records **that** a document exists and **where you keep it**. It does not store the file.

Your DPIA and privacy notice belong wherever your church already keeps its records — a counting system is a worse place for them, and the fewer copies of a document there are, the easier it is to know which one is current.
