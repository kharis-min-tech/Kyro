# Face matching & your data

::: tip Face data is temporary. It lasts one service and is then deleted.
Kyro keeps **no photographs of anybody**. Each face becomes a list of numbers, and Kyro has no idea whose it is — no names, no record of who attended, and nothing is ever compared against a list of people.

Those numbers exist for one reason: so that somebody who steps out and comes back is not counted twice. They are deleted **when the service ends** — and also when a different service starts, the moment face matching is switched off, and in any case **within twelve hours**, whether or not a service ever ended properly.

Nobody has to remember to delete anything. Kyro does it.
:::

This page explains exactly what face matching does, so your church can decide whether to use it and tell people what happens.

## What it is for

Kyro counts people without reading a single face, and that is what it does by default.

Face matching solves one problem that nothing else can: telling whether the person who just walked back in is somebody already counted. Without it:

- an usher who holds the door all morning is counted every time they step through;
- a choir in matching robes cannot be told apart at all, because Kyro falls back on what people are wearing;
- anybody who steps out for a phone call and returns is counted twice.

With it, each of those is one person.

## What Kyro actually keeps

| | |
|---|---|
| Photographs of faces | **None.** Ever. |
| Names | **None.** Kyro does not know who anybody is. |
| A record of who attended | **None.** |
| Comparison against a list of known people | **Never.** There is no such list. |
| A list of 128 numbers per face | Yes — for the length of one service |

The numbers describe the shape of a face well enough to say "this is the same person Kyro saw ten minutes ago". They cannot be turned back into a photograph, and they are no use to anybody without Kyro.

## When it is deleted

Five separate ways, and only one of them depends on anything working properly:

- when the service ends;
- when a different service starts;
- the moment face matching is switched off;
- whenever the counting computer is told to start a session fresh;
- and **within twelve hours regardless** — even if a service never ended properly, which can happen if a camera computer is switched off mid-morning.

That last one is the backstop. A promise about face data should not depend on everything else working.

## Switching it on

Cameras page → **Face matching** → **Switch on…**

An administrator confirms two things, and Kyro records who confirmed them and when:

1. a **data protection impact assessment** for face matching has been completed and approved by the church;
2. **notices are up at every entrance** saying cameras are used to count attendance, and how to opt out.

It is off until somebody does that, and it only ever runs **during a service** — Kyro records services by itself, see [Sessions](/pages/sessions).

## Telling people

Whatever a church decides about the law, people should be able to find out what is happening without having to ask. A notice at each entrance is the practical version of that, and it is why Kyro asks an administrator to confirm they are up.

Useful wording to adapt:

> *Cameras in this building count how many people are here. During services they also compare faces so that the same person is not counted twice. No photographs or names are kept, and the information is deleted when the service ends. Speak to a steward if you would prefer not to take part.*

## Deciding not to use it

Perfectly reasonable, and Kyro works without it.

You lose the ability to tell a returning person from a new one when no camera can see their face — so expect ushers, and anybody stepping out and back, to be counted more than once. Everything else works exactly the same: the headcount, arrivals and departures, the seat map, the rota, the analytics.

## If you want advice

Reading faces to tell people apart is biometric data under UK GDPR, and the rules are stricter than for counting heads. Whether, and on what basis, a church may do it is a decision for the church — Kyro cannot make it and does not pretend to.

The Information Commissioner's Office publishes free, readable guidance for small organisations, including on biometric data and on what a data protection impact assessment involves.
